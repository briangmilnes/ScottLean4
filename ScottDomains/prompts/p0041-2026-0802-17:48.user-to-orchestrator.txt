## turn — assistant 2026-08-02T17:49:32.615000-07:00

Right — and the "why not" is the important lesson about how `rw` works.

**`rw` is not search; it's first-match rewriting.** The lemma is
```
mul_comm : ∀ (a b : α), a * b = b * a
```
When you write `rw [mul_comm]` with no arguments, Lean takes the **left-hand side of the equation as a pattern** — `?a * ?b`, with `?a, ?b` as metavariables — and matches it against the goal. Two mechanical rules govern what happens:

1. It rewrites the **first occurrence** the pattern matches, in a fixed **outside-in, left-to-right traversal**. It does *not* look for "the occurrence that will make the proof go through" — it has no goal-directed intelligence here.
2. Once that first match fixes the metavariables (say `?a := a`, `?b := b`), it rewrites **every occurrence of that now-concrete term** `a * b` — not every product.

**Why that fails on the inner term.** Your goal is
```
a * b * c = b * (a * c)
```
and `a * b * c` parses as **`(a * b) * c`**. The *first* thing the pattern `?x * ?y` matches, going outside-in, is the **outer** product `(a * b) * c`. So bare `rw [mul_comm]` gives you
```
c * (a * b) = b * (a * c)     -- flipped the OUTER product, not what you wanted
```
It genuinely *did* find "a term" — just the outermost one, not the inner `a * b`. `mul_comm` can't know you meant the inner multiplication, because syntactically **every** product matches `?a * ?b`; the ambiguity is resolved by "first in traversal," not by intent.

**How to target the inner term** (any of these):
- **Give the arguments** — what the example does: `rw [mul_comm a b]` pins `?a := a, ?b := b`, so it rewrites exactly `a * b`.
- **Pick the occurrence:** `nth_rewrite 2 [mul_comm]` (or `nth_rw 2`) rewrites the *second* matching product instead of the first.
- **Navigate first:** `conv` to zoom into the subterm, then `rw [mul_comm]` there.
- **Skip the bookkeeping entirely:** `ring` normalizes any commutative-ring expression and closes `a * b * c = b * (a * c)` in one shot — you only fight with `rw` while *learning* how rewriting works.

So the short version: you *can* apply bare `mul_comm`, but it rewrites the **first (outermost) match**, and to hit an inner product you must disambiguate — with explicit arguments, `nth_rw`, or `conv`.
