## The proof split unevenly, in a useful way

**Directedness of `compactsBelow (x, y)` turned out to be free.** The join of two compacts below `(x, y)` is compact by the package's own `isCompactElement_of_isLUB_pair` — "a least upper bound of two compact elements is compact", stated for a bare `PartialOrder` with the LUB as a hypothesis — and is still below `(x, y)`. No product reasoning enters that half at all.

**Only the least-upper-bound half needed real work**, via `isCompactElement_prod`: given directed `s` with `IsLUB s u` and `(a,b) ≤ u`, compactness of `a` against the first-projection image gives `p₁ ∈ s` with `a ≤ p₁.1`, compactness of `b` gives `p₂ ∈ s` with `b ≤ p₂.2`, and directedness merges them. The two coordinates are found in **different** members of `s` — the same move `Currying.lean` makes when it uncurries.

Then for an upper bound `(u,v)` of `compactsBelow (x,y)`: every `a ∈ compactsBelow x` yields a compact `(a, ⊥) ≤ (x,y)`, hence `a ≤ u`, so `u` bounds `compactsBelow x` and `x ≤ u` by algebraicity of the first factor.

## One scoping decision

Stated for complete **lattices**, not arbitrary algebraic cpos. Over a general cpo the join need not exist, and directedness would instead need the *projection* direction — `(a,b)` compact implies `a` and `b` compact — which takes a separate argument through the image of `fun x => (x, b)`. `ALat` doesn't need it, so I didn't prove it; noted in the module in case the general case is ever wanted.
