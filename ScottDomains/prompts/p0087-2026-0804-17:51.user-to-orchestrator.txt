Yes — I read that as **"without the de Bruijn indices,"** and the answer is: absolutely, though the choice is really *which pain you'd rather have.* The current file uses de Bruijn's nameless indices (`var 0`, index-shifting `subst`) — which, fittingly, came out of **de Bruijn's Automath**, right in your intellectual neighborhood. The alternatives:

**1. Named variables + α-equivalence (the textbook way).** `lam x, t` with actual names. The catch is exactly what de Bruijn was escaping: substitution must avoid capture (renaming), and you must work modulo α — either by quotienting the term type or carrying α-equivalence everywhere. Confluence is provable, but the substitution lemmas get fiddly. This is the "honest" λ-calculus, and the most painful to mechanize.

**2. Locally nameless** (the modern pragmatic compromise). *Bound* variables as de Bruijn indices, *free* variables as names. α-equivalence is again just syntactic equality, but free variables stay readable, and you avoid the quotient. This is what most current Coq/Lean developments use for exactly this kind of metatheory.

**3. Nominal (Gabbay–Pitts).** Names with a built-in theory of permutations and freshness; α-conversion is handled once, structurally, by the framework. Elegant — it's how Isabelle/Nominal proves Church–Rosser — but it needs the nominal infrastructure underneath.

**4. HOAS / PHOAS.** Represent the binder with Lean's *own* function space: `lam : (Term → Term) → Term`. Then object-level substitution *is* meta-level application — **no substitution lemmas at all**. The price: the naive inductive isn't strictly positive (Lean rejects it), so you need PHOAS/weak-HOAS, and you must rule out "exotic" terms; confluence becomes subtle to even state.

The key point for **confluence specifically**: the *high-level* Tait–Martin-Löf argument (parallel reduction + diamond via complete developments) is essentially representation-independent — it's the **substitution lemmas underneath** that change. De Bruijn makes those into index-shifting arithmetic; named makes them capture-avoidance; locally-nameless makes them open/close. Same theorem, different plumbing.
