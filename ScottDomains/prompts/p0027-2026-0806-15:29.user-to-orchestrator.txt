## r0018 — the formalization decision, settled

"A fixed point operator is a **class** of continuous functions `F_D : (D → D) → D`" is not a function in Lean, it is a family over a universe. `FixedPointOperator.{u}` is a structure whose field takes a type `D : Type u` *and* a `CompletePartialOrder D` instance. Uniformity quantifies over two such types with a strict continuous map between. The risk was a universe bump; there is none — the subtype `↓fix(f)` the proof needs lives in the same `Type u` as `D`.

**I dropped one of the paper's hypotheses deliberately.** The paper asks each `F_D` to be continuous. The uniqueness argument never uses it, so it is not in the structure — including it would have *weakened* the theorem by quantifying over fewer operators.

The proof follows the paper: restrict to `D₀ = ↓fix(f)`, a cpo; `f` restricts because `x ⊑ fix f` gives `f x ⊑ f (fix f) = fix f`; the inclusion is strict, continuous, and commutes with `f` — all three by `rfl`, which is the payoff of making `D₀` a subtype with the induced order. Uniformity transports the value, and `f₀` has exactly one fixed point because Theorem 1 makes `fix f` the least one.

That dependency is why r0017 had to come first. Had the inventory's "Theorem 1 is Mathlib reuse" claim gone unchecked, this proof would have had no least-fixed-point property to appeal to in the paper's setting — `OrderHom.lfp` is over a complete lattice and would not have applied.
