An **`.olean`** is Lean's **compiled module file** — the "**o**bject file for **Lean**," analogous to a `.o` in C, `.pyc` in Python, or `.beam` in Erlang.

Concretely: when Lean processes a source module `Foo.lean`, it elaborates and type-checks it, producing an **environment** — the set of declarations that module adds (constants, definitions, theorems *with their proof terms*, their types, notation, attributes, instances). Lean **serializes that elaborated environment** to `Foo.olean` (a binary format, memory-mapped for speed). Then:

- On `import Foo`, Lean **loads `Foo.olean` directly** instead of re-reading and re-checking `Foo.lean`. You get all of Foo's definitions and theorems without re-elaborating them.
- That's the whole point: without oleans, every `import Mathlib…` would re-typecheck thousands of files each time. With them, it's a fast load of pre-built artifacts.

Three practical consequences we've been living with:
1. **Mathlib ships as oleans.** The "8,459 files" that just downloaded (**6.1 GB**) *are* Mathlib's `.olean` files. `lake exe cache get` fetches these prebuilt so you don't compile Mathlib from source (which would be hours of CPU).
2. **They're version-locked.** An `.olean` is tied to the exact Lean toolchain + the hashes of everything it imports. That's *exactly* why Beeson's **v4.32.2** oleans can't be reused for MIL's **v4.30.0** — different toolchain → incompatible oleans → each project needs its own set.
3. **They're the unit of caching/rebuild.** Change a source file and Lean rebuilds just its `.olean` (and dependents), not the world.

So "Mathlib is built" = "its `.olean` files are present on disk for this toolchain."
