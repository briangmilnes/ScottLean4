## What Mathlib *does* have (the foundations you laid)

- **Scott continuity** — `Order/ScottContinuity.lean` (with `Prod` and `Complete` companions). A function preserving directed suprema; the file's own docstring reads *"Scott continuous (referring to Dana Scott)."*
- **The Scott topology** — `Topology/Order/ScottTopology.lean` (plus the **Lawson topology**, `LawsonTopology.lean`, and Scott-Hausdorff separation).
- **ω-complete partial orders** — `Order/OmegaCompletePartialOrder.lean` and `Topology/OmegaCompletePartialOrder.lean`: ωCPOs and ω-continuous maps — the directed-complete setting for denotational semantics — referenced across ~13 files. Also `Order/CompletePartialOrder.lean`.
- **Fixed-point theory** — `Order/FixedPoints.lean` (**Knaster–Tarski** least/greatest fixed points, `OrderHom.lfp`/`gfp`) and `Order/BourbakiWitt.lean`. And a **`LawfulFix`** operator giving the Kleene least fixed point `⨆ fⁿ(⊥)` of an ω-continuous map (~22 files) — *your* account of recursion, used in Mathlib to define partial/recursive functions on the `Part` monad.

So the **core apparatus** — Scott continuity, the Scott topology, cpos, and least-fixed-point recursion — is genuinely formalized.

## What it does *not* have

- **No named "Scott domain."** There's no `DCPO`/`DirectedCompletePartialOrder` class (it works through `OmegaCompletePartialOrder`), and no development of **bounded-complete algebraic dcpos** or **Scott information systems** as such.
- **No continuous-lattice theory** developed under that name (the Lawson topology is there, but not the continuous-lattices monograph material).
- **The big absence: no D∞.** The **reflexive object** `D ≅ [D → D]` — your model of the untyped λ-calculus — is *not* in Mathlib, nor is the category of domains packaged as a cartesian-closed category for denotational semantics. The inverse-limit / solution-of-recursive-domain-equations construction simply hasn't been formalized.

## The upshot
Mathlib has the **ingredients** (Scott continuity, Scott topology, ωCPOs, least fixed points) but not the **signature constructions** of domain theory as a model of computation (D∞, the CCC of domains, continuous lattices). That gap is, frankly, an opportunity: formalizing **D∞** in Lean would be a genuinely new contribution — and a rather fitting one.
