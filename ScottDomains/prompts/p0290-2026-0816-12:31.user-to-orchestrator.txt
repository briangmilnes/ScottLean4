## What's actually proved

`Basic.lean` has no proof holes. The category `Equ` is real, not stubbed: `Hom A B` is a genuine `Quotient` of `Equivariant A B` by `MapEquiv`, and `id_comp`, `comp_id`, `assoc` are proved as equalities *of classes*. `comp_congr` — composition respects `MapEquiv` in both arguments — is what lets composition descend to the quotient; without it there's no category at all.

Two encoding points I made deliberately, both departures from the 1998 file we looked at earlier:

**`MapEquiv` uses the paper's two-point form**, `∀ x y. x ≡_A y → f x ≡_B g y`, not pointwise `f x ≡ g x`. The 1998 module used the diagonal reading, which is strictly weaker. The two-point form has a consequence worth the trouble: `MapEquiv f f` *is* equivariance of `f`, so `mapEquiv_refl` is literally `f.equivariant`. And `mapEquiv_trans` derives `A.Rel x x` from `A.Rel x y` by symmetry-then-transitivity rather than taking it from reflexivity — so the same proof survives the move to *partial* equivalence relations in Definition 3.11.

**Morphisms are equivalence classes.** Stating the laws up to `Eq` on representatives would be a different, false claim.

## Footnote 4 shaped the design

Because `Equ` is *not* well-powered, **no `WellPowered EquilogicalSpace` instance may be declared** — it sits right next to the completeness instances and looks natural, and declaring it would let typeclass resolution silently prove false things. That's why every name carries `regular`, and why the two regular halves of 3.10 are `Prop`-valued claims over `Small.{u}` rather than instances (Mathlib has no `RegularWellPowered`). Footnote 4 itself is `Theorem310NotWellPowered`, a claim `def` — the same call as in the 1998 file, for the same reason: Johnstone's counterexample isn't exhibited in the paper, so the `Small.{u}` encoding is unchecked and a `sorry` would assert it.
