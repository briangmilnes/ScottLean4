## r0022 — §3.2, and a gap I did not paper over

`EffectivePresentation` renders "effectively decidable" as `DecidablePred`, which is faithful: a `Decidable` instance in Lean *is* a deciding program. I deliberately did not use Mathlib's `ComputablePred`, which additionally ties the decision to a recursion-theoretic model — nothing in the paper's conditions 1 and 2 needs that. Both conditions sit on `ℕ` and `Finset ℕ` rather than on the domain, which is the whole point of a presentation: it moves decidability onto a countable index set where it can be asked at all.

**One thing is left out on purpose.** The paper's *computable function* needs `{m | eₘ ⊑ f(dₙ)}` to be recursively enumerable, and Mathlib v4.32.2 has **no `RePred` or equivalent** — `grep "def RePred"` across the library returns nothing. Building r.e. predicates is recursion theory, not domain theory. Approximating it with `Decidable` would be strictly stronger and therefore wrong, so I recorded the gap in the inventory rather than filling it badly.

The one elaboration failure was another instance of the ordering discipline you asked about: `countable_compacts` concludes `(compacts α).Countable`, which never mentions the presentation `d`, so Lean's automatic section-variable inclusion dropped it. `include d in` restores it — and must sit *before* the docstring, not between it and the declaration.
