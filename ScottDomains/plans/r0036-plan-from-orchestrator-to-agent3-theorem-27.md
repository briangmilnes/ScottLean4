## The goal

Theorem 27 — for any bounded complete domain `D` there is a projection
`p : U → D` — is proved **conditionally** on `main` as `Dyadic.thm27` and
`Dyadic.thm27_of_isNormallyRepresented` (r0034). Everything after the paper's
Boolean-algebra paragraph is Lean-checked. What is assumed is the one definition
at `ScottDomains/Dyadic.lean:447`:

    def IsNormallyRepresented (A : Type*) [PartialOrder A] : Prop :=
      ∃ N : Set U₀, IsNormalIn N (Set.univ : Set U₀) ∧ Nonempty (A ≃o ↥N)

**Discharge `IsNormallyRepresented ↥(compacts D)` for `D` a bounded complete
domain, and Theorem 27 becomes unconditional.** That single input is the whole
gap. Read the section docstring at `Dyadic.lean:388–436` first; it quotes the
paper's proof paragraph verbatim and states exactly where the split was made and
why.

## The route the paper takes

The paper's paragraph, quoted in that docstring:

1. `B = U₀ ∪ {∅}` is a Boolean algebra, and it is **atomless** — every dyadic
   interval contains a proper subinterval.
2. `B` is countable. Up to isomorphism the only countable atomless Boolean
   algebra is the free one on countably many generators (**Vaught's theorem**,
   proved by back-and-forth).
3. That algebra has every countable Boolean algebra isomorphic to a subalgebra.
4. For `A` a countable bounded complete poset, let `B'` be the Boolean algebra of
   subsets of `A` generated by the `↑x = {y ∈ A | x ⊑ y}`. Then `j : B' → B` maps
   `B'` isomorphically onto a subalgebra, and `j ∘ i` cuts down to an isomorphism
   between `A` and a normal subposet `A' ◁ U₀`.

Bounded completeness of `D` is spent in step 4 — to know a bounded family of the
`↑x` has non-empty intersection — not in the part already proved.

Row 3 is the asset. That file builds the back-and-forth as an *ideal of finite
partial isomorphisms*: `PartialIso` (line 106), `exists_across` (123),
`definedAtLeft`/`definedAtRight` (170, 190), `funOfIdeal`/`invOfIdeal` (206, 213),
then `embedding_from_countable_to_dense` (225) and `iso_of_countable_dense` (244).
**That is the same proof shape Vaught's theorem needs**, with "dense linear order
without endpoints" replaced by "atomless Boolean algebra" and finite partial
order-isomorphisms replaced by finite partial Boolean-algebra isomorphisms. Read
it as the template rather than inventing a scheme; and note that step 3 above is
`embedding_from_countable_to_dense`'s analogue, not `iso_of_countable_dense`'s —
what Theorem 27 actually consumes is the **embedding** of a countable Boolean
algebra into the atomless one, which is the weaker and cheaper of the two.

That last observation is the most valuable thing in this plan: **the full
uniqueness theorem may not be needed.** Vaught's theorem is how the *paper* gets
the embedding, but if the one-sided back-and-forth gives `B' ↪ B` directly, then
atomlessness of `B` plus countability of `B'` suffices, and the categoricity half
never has to be proved. Check this before committing to the harder statement.
