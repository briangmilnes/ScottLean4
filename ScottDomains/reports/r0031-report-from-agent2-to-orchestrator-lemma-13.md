# r0031 agent2 — Lemma 13, and the defect the plan predicted

## Headline

The obstacle the plan named **did materialize**, and it is the third occurrence
of the `ScottHom`/`Smash` defect class. `IdealCompletion.idealSup` returns `⊥` on
a family of ideals that is **bounded but not directed**, so the class-valued
claim `BoundedComplete (Powerdomain D)` is **false for all three powerdomains**.
That is proved by the kernel, not argued.

The paper's Lemma 13 is nonetheless **true**, and it is proved for both conjuncts
the paper states, in the paper's own wording. Nothing was weakened: the class
`BoundedComplete` asserts strictly more than §4.5's definition of *bounded
complete*, and it is the extra assertion that fails.

## Which of the paper's symbols each conjunct is

The plan's `D]` and `D[` are an ASCII rendering of the paper's `D♯` and `D♭` —
the **upper (Smyth)** and **lower (Hoare)** powerdomains of §5.2, quoted verbatim
in `Powerdomain/Smyth.lean`. So `lem13_smyth` is `D♯` and `lem13_hoare` is `D♭`.

There is **no `lem13_plotkin`**. The paper's third powerdomain is `D♮`, the
convex (Plotkin) one, and Lemma 13 does not name it. The order-theoretic reason
is already in the repository: `Plotkin.not_single_le_pair` shows `{a} ⊑ {a, b}`
fails under Egli–Milner, so the convex pre-order has no join for a bounded pair,
which is exactly the input both positive proofs consume. Preservation of bounded
completeness by the convex powerdomain is not claimed here in either direction.

## Why the class-valued form is false

`Domain.lean` states

    class BoundedComplete (α) [CompletePartialOrder α] : Prop where
      isLUB_sSup_of_bddAbove : ∀ s, BddAbove s → IsLUB s (sSup s)

so it asserts two things: that a bounded set *has* a least upper bound (§4.5's
definition, and Lemma 13), and that the ambient `sSup` *computes* it. For every
powerdomain the ambient `sSup` is `IdealCompletion.idealSup`, whose `dite`
branches on `Order.IsIdeal (⋃₀ …)` and returns `⊥` otherwise.

The plan's reading — that this branch is "the membership condition and therefore
correct" — is where the analysis has to be corrected. It is the membership
condition **for the union**, but the union is only the least upper bound when the
family is directed. For a merely bounded family the least upper bound is the
ideal *generated by* the union. `IdealCompletion.lean`'s own module docstring
states the discrepancy from the other side: *Mathlib's is the ideal generated by
the union, this one is the union itself and `⊥` elsewhere.* So the branch is on a
condition that is sufficient but not necessary — the same shape as `ScottHom`
(directedness instead of continuity of the pointwise supremum, r0006–r0011) and
`Smash` (directedness instead of landing in `NonBotPair`, r0027).

### The witness, in `P N`

`Powerset.lean` makes the compact elements of `P N` the finite subsets, so
`k0 = {0}`, `k1 = {1}` and `k01 = {0,1}` are compact, `k0` and `k1` are
incomparable, and `k01` is above both. For each powerdomain take

    S = { ↓{k0} , ↓{k1} }   ⊆   the powerdomain,

with `↓` the principal ideal of the corresponding one-element member of
`Pf(K(P N))`.

* `S` is **bounded above** by `↓{k01}`, in all three orderings.
* `⋃₀ S` is **not directed**: an element `w` of it satisfies `w ⊑ {k0}` or
  `w ⊑ {k1}`, and being above both `{k0}` and `{k1}` then forces `k0` and `k1`
  comparable. Under `⊑♭` and Egli–Milner the Hoare conjunct does it; under `⊑♯`
  the upper conjunct does.
* Hence `sSup S = ⊥` by `sSup_eq_bot_of_not_directedOn`, and `↓{k0} ≤ ⊥` would
  give `k0 ≤ ⊥`, i.e. `{0} ⊆ ∅`.

Two remarks on what this does and does not show. The family **does** have a least
upper bound — `genIdeal S` — so the mathematics of the powerdomain is not in
question; only the Lean rendering of a total `sSup` is. And the refutation is
robust: it uses nothing about `P N` beyond one incomparable pair of compacts with
an upper bound, so any bounded complete domain with a non-linear basis produces
the same failure.

### The repair, which I did not make

`IdealCompletion.lean` is a shared module and the plan forbids me to change it.
The repair that matches the two previous ones is to branch `idealSup` on the
condition that makes the candidate value the least upper bound rather than on a
sufficient condition for it: define `idealSup S` as the ideal **generated by**
`⋃₀ (SetLike.coe '' S)` whenever that ideal exists, falling back to `⊥`
otherwise. `genIdeal` in my file is that ideal, and `isIdeal_genIdeal` gives the
condition under which it is one; `exists_isLUB_of_bddAbove_idealCompletion` shows
the resulting value is correct on bounded families, and the existing
`lubOfDirected` proof would still go through on directed ones because
`genIdeal S = ⋃₀ S` there. That is a `IdealCompletion.lean` change plus a re-check
of everything downstream of it, and it belongs to whoever owns that module.

## How Lemma 13 is proved

One construction serves both conjuncts. For a family `S` of ideals over a
pre-order `A` bounded above by an ideal `J`,

    genIdeal S = {c ∈ A | c belongs to every ideal that contains every member of S}.

Lower-closedness and `⊥ ∈ genIdeal S` are immediate. Directedness is the whole
content and is where boundedness is spent: two members `a, b` lie in `J`, so
`J`'s directedness bounds `{a, b}` in `A`; if bounded pairs of `A` have least
upper bounds, the join `c` lies in every ideal `K` above `S` — `K` bounds `a` and
`b` by some `e ∈ K`, so `c ≤ e`, so `c ∈ K` by downward closure — hence in
`genIdeal S`. Being the least ideal containing every member of `S`, it is their
least upper bound. Only **binary joins of bounded pairs** are needed; no finite
joins, no transfinite closure.

That reduces each conjunct to a statement about `Pf(K(D))`:

* **`D♭`** (`hoare_exists_isLUB_pair`): under `⊑♭` the join is `u ∪ v` and exists
  **unconditionally**, because every element of `u` is below itself in `u ∪ v`.
  So the Hoare pre-order is a join-semilattice and `[BoundedComplete α]` is never
  consumed — `D♭` is bounded complete for *every* domain `D`. I kept the paper's
  hypothesis on the statement and recorded the strengthening in the docstring
  rather than dropping it.
* **`D♯`** (`smyth_exists_isLUB_pair`): under `⊑♯` the join of a bounded pair is
  `{a ⊔ b | a ∈ u, b ∈ v, {a,b} bounded in D}`, and here both halves of the
  hypothesis are spent. `[BoundedComplete α]` supplies `a ⊔ b`;
  `isCompactElement_of_isLUB_pair` (`Domain.lean`, already present) certifies it
  is again compact, so the join is a point of `Pf(K(D))`. Boundedness of `{u, v}`
  is what makes the set non-empty, which the paper's `Pf` requires — an upper
  bound `w` is non-empty, and any `c ∈ w` selects `a ∈ u`, `b ∈ v` bounded by `c`.
