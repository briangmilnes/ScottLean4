### 3.2 The defect, confirmed by hand and not taken from r0041

Using only the paper's own §5.1 definitions — `u ⊢♯ v` iff `(∀x ∈ u)(∃y ∈ v). x ⊒
y`; `u ⊢♭ v` iff `(∀y ∈ v)(∃x ∈ u). x ⊒ y`; `u ⊢♮ v` iff both — take `u = {1}`
and `v = {1, ⊥}` in `P*f(N⊥)`.

* `u ⊢♯ v`: the only `x ∈ u` is `1`, and `1 ⊒ 1` with `1 ∈ v`. Holds.
* `u ⊢♭ v`: for `y = 1` take `x = 1`; for `y = ⊥` take `x = 1`, since `1 ⊒ ⊥` in
  the flat order. Holds.

So `u ⊢♮ v`. But `⊥ ∉ u`, so `u ⊇ v` fails, and `u ≠ v`. The printed
disjunction is false at a pair the relation holds of. **Clause 1 is false as
printed.** r0041's report is right, and this is now checked twice, independently.

I also verified the repair in both directions rather than accepting it. With
clause 1 replaced by `⊥ ∈ v ∧ v ∖ {⊥} ⊆ u`, the biconditional holds: (⇐) when
`⊥ ∈ v` the `⊢♯` conjunct is free because every element sits above `⊥ ∈ v`, and
the `⊢♭` conjunct is exactly `v ∖ {⊥} ⊆ u` plus non-emptiness of `u` for `y = ⊥`;
(⇒) when `⊥ ∈ v`, `⊢♭` plus "nothing is strictly above a non-`⊥` point" forces
each non-`⊥` member of `v` into `u`; when `⊥ ∉ v`, the same fact collapses both
conjuncts to inclusions and `u = v`. That is `plotkin_le_iff`'s content, and the
argument uses no finiteness — which is what licenses `Ple` extending it to
infinite sets for row 19.

### 3.3 Why the label is `S≠` and not `S+P`

The property, read literally off the page, is

    P₁₈ ≔ ∀ u v ∈ P*f(N⊥), (u ⊢♮ v ↔ (⊥ ∈ v ∧ u ⊇ v) ∨ u = v).

Applying agent2's three-step test, which the orchestrator did not overturn:

1. **`plotkin_le_iff` is not `P₁₈`.** Its type is `P₁₈` with clause 1's `u ⊇ v`
   replaced by `∀ a ∈ v, a ≠ ⊥ → a ∈ u`. That is a repaired form, and `S≠`'s own
   definition names "a repaired form" as its central case.
2. **`plotkin_printed_clause_one_fails` is not `¬P₁₈` either.** Its type is
   `∃ u v : Plotkin.FinCompacts NatBot, v ≤ u ∧ ¬(printed disjunction)` — it is
   `¬P₁₈` **at a witness**. It entails `¬P₁₈`, and entailment is not statement.
3. **`N` would be a worse error than `S+P`.** Row 18 is the only row in my range
   whose truth value the kernel has *decided*. Filing it with row 24, about which
   the development says nothing, would destroy the measurement.

So `S≠`, and it is `S≠` plus a kernel-checked refutation — the same shape as
Lemma 9.3, Lemma 9.5 and Theorem 16.2, and it should carry whatever label the
orchestrator settles on for that class, applied to all four rows at once. I have
not invented a new one.

**The refutation passes agent2's safety condition, and passes it by
construction.** Agent2 required that the witness leave the *corrected* law
standing, so that the separation is specific to the misprint and not an artefact
of a degenerate carrier. Here the proof of `plotkin_printed_clause_one_fails`
establishes `fcOneBot ≤ fcOne` **through** `plotkin_le_iff.mpr (Or.inl …)`
(`:730`) — it uses the corrected clause to produce the very relation that the
printed clause then fails to explain. The corrected law is therefore not merely
untouched by the witness; it is what the witness is built from. This is a
stronger guarantee than Lemma 9's, where the condition had to be checked
